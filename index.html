May to Sep 2026MA dissertation

The Localization Playbook: luxury branding in the GCC

How Louis Vuitton, Dior, Chanel, Cartier, Bvlgari and Hermès adapt for Gulf customers after Saudi Vision 2030, and where adaptation tips over into appropriation.

Brands
Six maisons
Period
2022 to 2026
Status
Submitted September 2026
Read the full projectClose

Luxury spending in the GCC is growing fast. Mordor Intelligence projects it rising from $19.7B in 2025 to $36.1B by 2031, and European houses have answered with flagships, Ramadan capsules and Saudi ambassadors. That sits awkwardly with Kapferer's anti-laws of luxury, which treat localisation as a threat to a brand's mystique.

Not all localisation undermines mystique. The question is where adaptation ends and appropriation begins.

I used three methods:

  • Content analysis of the six houses across product, retail, ambassadors and digital.
  • Netnography (Kozinets) of public comments on official brand posts across X, Meta and TikTok, coded in NVivo.
  • A bilingual English/Arabic survey of GCC consumers, scoring campaigns from appropriation to authenticity.

As a Pakistani studying European luxury with a focus on the Gulf, I came to it as both an insider and an outsider, which shaped the netnography.

Jan to May 2026The Business of Luxury

Bremont: a 2027 campaign in three acts

A team consultancy brief for the British watchmaker's US push, built around the Terra Nova collection and a new Wayfinder identity.

Budget
£3.2M
Markets
US and UK
Delivered
Report and pitch deck
Read the full projectClose

Bremont is credible on craft and aviation heritage but weaker at keeping clients after the first purchase. We built the strategy to fix retention as much as acquisition, placing Bremont at the centre of a wider British luxury ecosystem.

  • Act 001, The Exhibit. Heritage installations at Heathrow, JFK, Grand Central and Waterloo: wartime aircraft in glass cases alongside Terra Nova watches.
  • Act 002, The Collective. A travelling pop-up across New York, Miami, Dallas and Los Angeles with Aston Martin, Hackett London, Crockett & Jones and Globe-Trotter.
  • Act 003, The Wayfinder Weekends. A members-only outdoor programme for Bremont owners, led by adventurers and mountaineers.

Grounded in Pine & Gilmore's Experience Economy and Moore's brand ecosystem theory.

Hana to Heiwa at Mitsukoshi Nihonbashi

"Flowers and Peace": a six-day display event for the Tokyo flagship, running from Hinamatsuri to International Women's Day 2027.

Place
Tokyo
Length

Concept partners

Read the full projectClose

The concept carries a political story about women and succession entirely through space, visual merchandising and brand collaboration, never through slogans.

Display is treated as a message, not simply as décor.

Visitors move from a chrysanthemum facade to a hinadan in the Central Hall beneath the 1930 pipe organ, then to a Leadership Gallery ending at an empty plinth labelled "2027". A corridor of tsurushi-bina hanging dolls, paper cranes and cherry blossom runs from cosmetics to jewellery.

The work covered the facade, three interior zones, brand collaborations, a PR plan with Tokyo Metro posters and social tiles, and staff styling.

Fabergé: a recovery plan for a Tsar's jeweller

A business report on Fabergé under Gemfields ownership, with a three-part strategy for 2026 to 2030.

Fabergé has imperial heritage and direct access to responsibly sourced coloured gemstones. By 2024 it was also seeing revenue fall 15% year on year, and customers asking "is this the real Fabergé?"

  • Financial focus: selective divestment and leaner operations.
  • Brand clarity: experiential retail and private client work in key cities.
  • Provenance: blockchain-backed tracking and imperial storytelling for buyers who want ethical, meaningful luxury.
Nov 2025 to Jan 2026Principles of Luxury

The Cagliostro Universe

A luxury cigar house I built from nothing: name, story, four collections, website mockups, an Instagram plan and a brand film concept.

Read the full projectClose

Cagliostro runs on what I called a "digital velvet rope": withholding information on purpose, so the brand stays a little out of reach. It draws on Kapferer's anti-laws, Veblen, and Bourdieu's idea that people carry several identities at once.

The four collections are Signature, Grand Cru, Aniversario and Millennium, each with its own character, from creamy to intense.

Nov 2025 to Jan 2026Context of Luxury

Why is luxury a contested issue?

An essay on class, identity and menswear, from Beau Brummell and the Teddy Boys to Virgil Abloh at Louis Vuitton.

Read the full projectClose

Using Bourdieu's habitus and cultural capital, the essay follows how luxury has marked class, and how Gen Z values and Abloh's "zeroing" approach unsettled that. It ends on quiet luxury, arguing that rejecting logos is still a way of signalling status, just a quieter one.

Luxury is not simply a category of goods. It is a symbolic system through which social hierarchies are reproduced, disrupted and renegotiated. From my MA essay, Why is luxury a contested issue?
